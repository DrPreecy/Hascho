# Product brief

## Vision

Create a modern, reusable workbench that helps one learning Project Owner develop many different kinds of ideas into well-reasoned projects, products, services, software, or businesses. The workbench should guide professional-quality thinking without taking ownership of material decisions and without imposing unnecessary bureaucracy.

Each project gets an independent workbench instance. The system adapts around the project while retaining a stable universal base. Provider changes must not destroy project memory.

## Intended journeys

The workbench may support:

- capturing spontaneous thoughts, including mobile and voice input;
- exploring ideas and alternative directions;
- framing problems, users, contexts, markets, and ecosystems;
- mapping assumptions, uncertainty, evidence, contradictions, and risk;
- conducting and evaluating research;
- preparing transparent decisions;
- developing concepts, business models, products, services, brands, and positioning;
- evaluating technical feasibility, security, privacy, legal, financial, and operational concerns;
- designing validation and experiments;
- planning and building software or non-software outcomes;
- preparing market entry and launch;
- operating, measuring, learning, scaling, pivoting, pausing, or ending responsibly.

No idea is required to become a business.

## Human-led, AI-supported

The Project Owner decides. AI may explain, research, connect, challenge, generate alternatives, recommend, summarize, and help execute approved work. AI must not silently convert assumptions into decisions.

Consequential decisions require a visible question, evidence, options, trade-offs, recommendation, uncertainty, human choice, and recorded rationale.

## Adaptive rather than linear

The design must show:

- dependencies and parallel work;
- required inputs and produced outputs;
- downstream effects;
- optional or simplified work;
- module activation conditions;
- iteration and return paths;
- pause, discard, reshape, and continue criteria;
- measurable progress and evidence;
- preservation of open, paused, contradictory, and rejected paths.

A lifecycle can orient the user, but active loops and epistemic status must remain separate concepts.

## Interaction requirements

Investigate modes such as Capture, Reflect, Explore, Research, Challenge, Connect, Converge, Decide, Design, Validate, Plan, Build, Review, Pause, and Resume.

For every retained mode define:

- when it is useful;
- the human role;
- AI assistance;
- possible input;
- guiding questions;
- affected artifacts;
- exit and switching conditions;
- protection against treating open thinking as concluded.

The interaction must adapt to project state, uncertainty, energy, device, time, and task. It must not become a long questionnaire.

## Continuity and quality of life

The user should always be able to see:

- the current project and work context;
- the current question;
- the last meaningful event and thought;
- the next sensible action or needed decision;
- active, blocked, paused, and stale work;
- open assumptions and available evidence;
- trustworthy results and their basis;
- rejected paths and their rationale;
- authoritative artifacts and potential invalidation.

Investigate quick capture, mobile and voice use, low-energy and deep-work modes, focus, resumability, progressive disclosure, contradiction detection, duplicate-work prevention, related insight connections, and comprehensible progress.

## Transformation structure

The Project Owner values a clear, visible transformation with an understandable sequence of major steps. The workbench should make movement from an early thought to a real outcome comprehensible.

The sequence must be universalized rather than copied from one industry. It should provide orientation, inputs, outputs, gates, and return paths while allowing project-specific modules, parallel work, iteration, pausing, and termination. A sequence is an orientation spine, not a rigid wizard.

## Software-project thematic coverage

When a project contains software, the system should help consider relevant areas such as browser and language fundamentals, developer workflow, backend and domain logic, data modeling, frontend and full-stack behavior, testing, security, systems, architecture, delivery and reliability, AI engineering, and product engineering.

The supplied software roadmap is a thematic reference only. It must not become a learning tracker, mandatory curriculum, fixed stage order, or requirement that every software project use every topic. Coverage is selected by actual scope, dependencies, risk, and architecture.

## Guidance-layer quality of life

Quality of life includes an active but human-controlled guidance layer:

- visible research, validation, project, implementation, review, or launch plans when relevant;
- context-aware next actions;
- optional prompt and action suggestions;
- clearly differentiated to-dos, questions, decisions, assumptions, experiments, risks, and reviews;
- agent scope and activity visibility;
- easy handoff, pause, and resume;
- verification guidance and evidence-backed completion.

Generated plans and prompts are proposals and conveniences, not hidden decisions or durable state by themselves.

## Responsible AI-assisted implementation

Software modules require proportional anti-vibe-coding safeguards: intent before code, repository inspection, bounded plans, understandable changes, explicit architecture decisions, real verification, security and data-integrity checks, honest uncertainty, and human approval at consequential or external boundaries.

## Candidate knowledge objects

Research before finalizing relationships among Project, Idea, Thread, Question, Assumption, Evidence, Source, Insight, Hypothesis, Experiment, Alternative, Decision, Risk, Requirement, Task, Artifact, Metric, Milestone, and Gate.

These are candidates, not a mandatory final schema.

## Multi-project boundary

The universal framework can be reused across projects, but each project remains independent. Larger projects may contain subprojects, workstreams, or linked questions. Cross-project patterns and methods can be reusable without mixing private project state or turning everything into one monolith.

## Visual reasoning

Different cognitive questions require different views. Investigate lifecycle roadmaps, current-context views, journey maps, Kanban, decision maps, assumption-evidence matrices, knowledge graphs, timelines, experiment boards, service blueprints, system architecture, dependency maps, risk matrices, metric trees, branching paths, and project health views.

The final process architecture and roadmap must be high-quality visual artifacts, not only long Markdown lists. A visual Control Room is a strong direction for orientation, not the only view and not automatically the data model.

## Agents, skills, and automation

Determine capabilities before deciding agent count. Prefer one context-aware assistant plus reusable skills unless isolation, parallel review, or specialized expertise has a clear benefit.

Every agent must expose sources, assumptions, uncertainty, and boundaries. Conflicting recommendations require comparison and human resolution. External actions and material decisions require appropriate approval.

## Later technical principles

When software design is authorized, consider portable state, provider adapters, versioned artifacts, provenance, standard interfaces, separation of domain logic, AI providers and UI, export, Git auditability, local and hosted options, permissions, backup, restoration, machine readability, and human readability.

Do not select a stack until usage, requirements, and architecture drivers are understood.

## Non-goals

Do not produce:

- a generic startup checklist;
- an industry-specific framework;
- a rigid wizard;
- a chatbot plus prompts;
- an autonomous business builder;
- an enterprise process for its own sake;
- a folder system presented as a product;
- a shallow template;
- disconnected methods;
- a system forcing every idea into a business;
- a provider-locked architecture;
- a single giant interface holding every project.

## Quality test

Every component must improve a decision, reduce uncertainty, preserve knowledge, expose risk, ease work, raise quality, secure continuity, or enable real progress. If it does none of these, remove it.
